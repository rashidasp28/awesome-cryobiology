    Cryobiology Glossary¶

    A beginner-friendly glossary of key terms used in cryobiology, cryopreservation, cryomicroscopy, cryoengineering, and low-temperature biology.

    A¶

    Antifreeze Proteins¶

    Ice-binding proteins found in some organisms that can modify ice crystal growth and inhibit ice recrystallization.

    Annealing¶

    A period during which a frozen sample is held at a specific subzero temperature. Annealing can promote ice crystal growth or recrystallization depending on conditions.

    B¶

    Biobanking¶

    The organized collection, processing, storage, and management of biological samples for research, clinical, conservation, or therapeutic use.

    C¶

    Controlled-Rate Freezing¶

    A freezing approach where temperature is reduced according to a defined cooling profile, often to reduce intracellular ice formation and osmotic injury.

    Cryoinjury¶

    Biological damage caused by cooling, freezing, storage at low temperature, or warming. Cryoinjury may involve ice formation, osmotic stress, membrane damage, oxidative stress, and rewarming damage.

    Cryomicroscopy¶

    Microscopy performed during cooling, freezing, storage, or warming to observe ice formation, cell response, phase transitions, or cryoinjury in real time.

    Cryoprotective Agent¶

    A chemical or biomolecule that protects cells, tissues, or biological materials from freezing or vitrification damage. Examples include DMSO, glycerol, ethylene glycol, trehalose, and sucrose.

    Cryostage¶

    A temperature-controlled stage mounted on a microscope to enable imaging under low-temperature conditions.

    D¶

    Devitrification¶

    The formation of ice crystals from a previously vitrified glassy state, usually during warming. Devitrification can damage biological samples.

    Directional Freezing¶

    A freezing method where a sample experiences a controlled thermal gradient, producing a moving freezing front.

    DMSO¶

    Dimethyl sulfoxide, a widely used permeating cryoprotective agent for mammalian cells and cell therapy products.

    G¶

    Glass Transition¶

    The temperature range where a liquid-like vitrified material transitions into a glassy solid state without crystallization.

    Glycerol¶

    A permeating cryoprotective agent commonly used in sperm and red blood cell cryopreservation.

    I¶

    Ice Nucleation¶

    The initial formation of an ice crystal nucleus. Ice nucleation may occur spontaneously or be induced intentionally.

    Ice Recrystallization¶

    The growth of larger ice crystals at the expense of smaller ones during storage or warming. It can contribute to cryoinjury.

    Intracellular Ice Formation¶

    Ice formation inside cells. It is often lethal or highly damaging because it can disrupt membranes, organelles, and intracellular structures.

    M¶

    Membrane Integrity¶

    The structural and functional state of a cell membrane. It is commonly assessed using dyes such as propidium iodide or SYBR-14/PI combinations.

    O¶

    Osmotic Stress¶

    Cellular stress caused by water movement across membranes in response to changes in solute concentration.

    R¶

    Rewarming Injury¶

    Damage that occurs during thawing or warming, including osmotic shock, devitrification, recrystallization, and thermal stress.

    S¶

    Solution Effects Injury¶

    Cryoinjury caused by increasing solute concentration as extracellular ice forms and excludes solutes into the remaining unfrozen fraction.

    Splat Cooling Assay¶

    A method used to evaluate ice recrystallization inhibition activity by rapidly freezing a small droplet and monitoring ice crystal growth during annealing.

    T¶

    Thermal Gradient¶

    A spatial temperature difference across a sample or device. Thermal gradients are central to directional freezing and cryostage engineering.

    Thawing Rate¶

    The rate at which a frozen sample is warmed. Thawing rate can strongly influence survival and recrystallization risk.

    V¶

    Vitrification¶

    A cryopreservation approach where a sample becomes a glass-like solid without ice crystal formation, usually requiring high CPA concentrations and rapid cooling.
