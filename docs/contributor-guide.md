# Contributor Guide

Thank you for contributing to Awesome Cryobiology.

This project aims to build an open, structured, reproducible, and community-driven knowledge ecosystem for cryobiology, cryomicroscopy, cryoengineering, and AI-assisted low-temperature biology.

Contributions from researchers, students, engineers, microscopists, clinicians, and computational scientists are welcome.

---

# Ways to Contribute

Potential contribution areas include:

- educational resources,
- landmark papers,
- review articles,
- datasets,
- segmentation workflows,
- cryostage designs,
- fluorescence imaging protocols,
- reproducible analysis pipelines,
- and scientific figures.

---

# Recommended Contribution Categories

## Papers

Potential additions:

- landmark studies,
- open-access reviews,
- engineering papers,
- cryomicroscopy papers,
- and AI-assisted imaging studies.

Recommended information:

- title,
- authors,
- DOI,
- open-access link,
- and short summary.

---

## Datasets

Recommended dataset information:

- acquisition conditions,
- imaging modality,
- annotations,
- metadata,
- licensing,
- and benchmark metrics.

Preferred formats:

- TIFF
- OME-TIFF
- OME-Zarr
- PNG
- JSON annotations

---

## Protocols

Recommended protocol structure:

- objective,
- materials and reagents,
- workflow steps,
- imaging configuration,
- quality control,
- and troubleshooting.

---

## Figures and Diagrams

Recommended figure categories:

- cryoinjury pathways,
- vitrification workflows,
- cryostage engineering,
- fluorescence workflows,
- and AI segmentation pipelines.

Preferred formats:

- SVG
- PNG
- PDF

---

# Reproducibility Standards

Recommended practices:

- metadata preservation,
- parameter logging,
- version-controlled analysis,
- and transparent reporting.

Potential metadata:

- imaging conditions,
- thermal parameters,
- fluorophores,
- CPA composition,
- and segmentation settings.

---

# Citation and Attribution

Please:

- properly cite original work,
- acknowledge contributors,
- respect licensing conditions,
- and prioritize open-access resources when possible.

---

- scientific rigor,

- and educational accessibility.
