# Multiphoton Cryomicroscopy

Multiphoton cryomicroscopy combines:

- nonlinear optical imaging,
- cryobiology,
- thermal engineering,
- fluorescence microscopy,
- and computational image analysis.

This approach enables real-time visualization of cryoinjury and low-temperature biological processes with reduced phototoxicity and improved optical sectioning.

---

# Principles of Multiphoton Excitation

Multiphoton microscopy relies on the near-simultaneous absorption of two or more lower-energy photons.

Advantages include:

- deeper optical penetration,
- reduced out-of-focus excitation,
- localized fluorescence generation,
- and reduced photodamage outside the focal plane.

Common excitation wavelengths:

- 780 nm
- 920 nm
- 1064 nm

# Cryogenic Imaging Challenges

Cryogenic imaging introduces several technical constraints:

## Thermal Drift

Temperature gradients can affect:
- focus stability,
- sample positioning,
- and optical alignment.

---

## Frost Formation

Condensation and frost can:
- scatter excitation light,
- reduce image quality,
- and interfere with fluorescence detection.

Potential mitigation strategies:
- dry gas purging,
- vacuum shrouds,
- environmental isolation,
- and anti-condensation shielding.

---

## Objective Thermal Exchange

Microscope objectives may introduce:
- local warming,
- thermal perturbation,
- and sample instability.

Water immersion systems require additional thermal consideration during low-temperature imaging.

---

# Fluorophore Compatibility

Cryogenic conditions may alter:

- fluorescence intensity,
- emission stability,
- photobleaching behavior,
- and spectral separation.

Representative fluorophores:

| Fluorophore | Typical Application |
| --- | --- |
| Propidium Iodide (PI) | Membrane-compromised cells |
| Calcein AM | Viable cells |
| CellMask Orange | Membrane labeling |
| DAPI | Nuclear staining |
| mBCl | Glutathione detection |

---

# Thermal Gradient Cryostages

Thermal-gradient cryostages enable:

- directional freezing,
- controlled thermal gradients,
- real-time cryoinjury imaging,
- and freezing-front tracking.

Important design considerations:

- optical access,
- thermal stability,
- sensor integration,
- and microscope compatibility.

---

# Image Acquisition Considerations

Important parameters include:

- laser power,
- pixel dwell time,
- detector gain,
- spectral filtering,
- averaging,
- and thermal equilibration.

Careful parameter reporting improves reproducibility.
