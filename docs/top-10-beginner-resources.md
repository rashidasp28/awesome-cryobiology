# Top 10 Beginner Resources

This reading path introduces cryobiology through ten complementary resources. It moves from core physical principles to laboratory practice, clinical applications, and computational analysis.

The list is intentionally short. Each resource was selected for scientific credibility, educational value, and coverage of a distinct part of the field. Access conditions can change, so check the linked publisher or institution before relying on local copies.

## Suggested order

### 1. Cryopreservation: An Overview of Principles and Cell-Specific Considerations

**Focus:** General introduction

Whaley and colleagues provide an accessible overview of cryopreservation history, physical principles, cryoprotective agents, and cell-specific considerations. Start here to build a shared vocabulary before reading the more specialized papers below.

- [Open-access review (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7995302/)
- Citation: Whaley D, Damyar K, Witek RP, Mendoza A, Alexander M, Lakey JR. *Cell Transplantation*. 2021.

### 2. Freezing of Living Cells: Mechanisms and Implications

**Focus:** Water transport, ice formation, and cryoinjury

Mazur's landmark review explains how cooling rate, cellular dehydration, intracellular ice, and prolonged exposure to concentrated solutions interact during freezing. Use it to understand why an optimal cooling rate depends on the biological system.

- [Abstract and citation record (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6383068/)
- Citation: Mazur P. *American Journal of Physiology*. 1984.

### 3. Principles of Cryopreservation

**Focus:** Core cryobiology framework

Pegg gives a concise foundation for controlled cooling, cryoprotectant use, storage, and recovery. Read this after Mazur to connect the underlying mechanisms to practical protocol design.

- [Abstract and citation record (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25428001/)
- Citation: Pegg DE. *Methods in Molecular Biology*. 2015.

### 4. Cryoprotectant Toxicity: Facts, Issues, and Questions

**Focus:** Cryoprotective agents

This review separates chemical toxicity from osmotic, thermal, and handling effects that can be confused with toxicity. Use it to learn why cryoprotectant concentration, temperature, exposure time, and addition or removal steps must be considered together.

- [Open-access review (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4620521/)
- Citation: Best BP. *Rejuvenation Research*. 2015.

### 5. Principles of Cryopreservation by Vitrification

**Focus:** Vitrification and warming

Fahy and Wowk introduce glass formation, critical cooling and warming behavior, cryoprotectant concentration, and the risks of crystallization during cooling or rewarming. This is a conceptual resource, not a universal protocol.

- [Abstract and citation record (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25428002/)
- Citation: Fahy GM, Wowk B. *Methods in Molecular Biology*. 2015.

### 6. High-Speed Video Cryomicroscopy for Measurement of Intracellular Ice Formation Kinetics

**Focus:** Cryomicroscopy

Karlsson describes a cryomicroscope setup and methods for observing intracellular ice formation. Read it as an example of how imaging, temperature control, and quantitative analysis can connect visible freezing events to cryobiological mechanisms.

- [Abstract and citation record (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32797414/)
- Citation: Karlsson JOM. *Methods in Molecular Biology*. 2021.

### 7. ISBER Best Practices for Repositories

**Focus:** Biobanking and specimen governance

The International Society for Biological and Environmental Repositories provides best-practice guidance for repository operations. Use it to broaden a protocol-focused view into quality management, documentation, storage, transport, ethics, and lifecycle stewardship.

- [ISBER Best Practices](https://www.isber.org/page/BPR/ISBER-Best-Practices-For-Repositories.htm)
- Publisher: International Society for Biological and Environmental Repositories.

### 8. Cryopreservation as a Key Element in the Successful Delivery of Cell-Based Therapies

**Focus:** Cell therapy preservation

This open review follows the cell-therapy workflow from source material and cryoprotection through storage, transport, thawing, and recovery. It helps beginners see why post-thaw function and manufacturing consistency matter beyond immediate viability.

- [Open-access review (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7727450/)
- Citation: Meneghel J, Kilbride P, Morris GJ. *Frontiers in Medicine*. 2020.

### 9. WHO Laboratory Manual for the Examination and Processing of Human Semen, Sixth Edition

**Focus:** Reproductive cryobiology and laboratory quality

Chapter 6 covers sperm cryopreservation within a wider laboratory-quality framework. Use the manual to learn how specimen handling, examination, quality control, and reporting surround a reproductive cryopreservation procedure.

- [WHO publication record](https://iris.who.int/items/7781ad55-85b0-409a-8e3a-79c9eb415d7c)
- Publisher: World Health Organization. 2021.

### 10. Cellpose 2.0: How to Train Your Own Model

**Focus:** Computational and image analysis

Cellpose 2.0 is a practical introduction to adapting a generalist cell-segmentation model to new image types. Use it after learning basic microscopy and experimental design, and validate any model against representative expert-annotated cryomicroscopy images before drawing biological conclusions.

- [Research article (Nature Methods)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-022-01663-4)
- Citation: Pachitariu M, Stringer C. *Nature Methods*. 2022.

## How to use this path

For every resource, record:

1. the biological material and preservation goal;
2. cooling, warming, and storage conditions;
3. cryoprotectant composition and exposure;
4. the measured outcome and its time point;
5. the controls, sample size, and sources of uncertainty;
6. whether the method can be reproduced with the information provided.

A published method is not automatically suitable for another cell type, tissue, device, or clinical setting. Treat these resources as a foundation for critical reading, then consult application-specific protocols, institutional requirements, and current standards before designing an experiment.
